Board of directors
==================

Being a Swedish company, we are required to have a board of directors. In most companies, the board is appointed by the owners to represent them so that the management group, headed by the CEO, runs the company according to the directives from the owners and the board.

At Crisp, this works a little differently. First of all, [we invite all Crispers to become owners after 2 years](ownership-model.html), and second, we hold a yearly election of the board members where all Crispers (owners or not) have the right to vote. Since [we have gotten rid of the CEO role](decisions.html), and since we have very autonomous consultants, there is really quite little to manage.

What does the board do?
-----------------------

The board have monthly board meetings. The meetings are open for any Crispers to attend if they wish.

The board is responsible for:

-   **Finances** - keeping an eye on the liquidity to be able to do day-to-day business, and aiming for a zero result at the end of the fiscal year. The board manages the bank account continuously, without any up-front budgeting process (see [Crisplets](crisplets.html)). See [Economic Model](economic-model.html) for details.
-   **The Core fee** - setting the fee every Crisper pays, so that it covers the cost of [Crisp Core](core.html). Opt-in services that are run as [Crisplets](crisplets.html), such as Sales, Engineering and Office, set their own fees. Where a Crisplet also does something for all Crispers, like Sales and Office do, Core pays it a contribution for that.
-   **The accounting firm** - an external accounting firm handles our invoicing, payments, bookkeeping and Crisplet Accounts. The board oversees it.
-   **Legal duties and contracts** - the duties Swedish law puts on a board, such as the annual report. The board signs some of Crisp's contracts; our sales professional signs others.
-   **Happiness** - if there is any Crisper with a low score on [Happiness Index](happiness-index.html), a board member takes it upon themselves to call that person and to try and find out what can be done to make the Crisper happier.

Board work is paid. Each board member gets a fixed fee per year for their board work, covered by the Core fee like the rest of [Crisp Core](core.html).

Some things are deliberately *not* the board's job. The unconference planners and facilitators are elected at each [unconference](unconference.html), onboarding of new Crispers is done by a Crisper who volunteers, and the office is run by the Office Crisplet. See [Admin and day-to-day operations](admin.html).

This is the template for our monthly board meetings.

1.  Choice of chair, secretary and minutes approver
2.  Review of the finances
3.  General information and [Crisplet](crisplets.html) updates
4.  Course bookings
5.  Consultant utilization
6.  Review of the [Happiness Index](happiness-index.html)
    -   The board creates action points to call any Crispers that have a low score on Happiness Index.
7.  Next meeting
8.  Important upcoming events, if any
9.  The board's ongoing work
10. Any other business
11. Topics for the next unconference
12. New decisions to add to the decision log

How do we elect the board?
--------------------------

We elect the board annually at the [Crisp Unconference](unconference.html) in the fall. Swedish law requires the board to be formally elected at the annual shareholders' meeting, but that meeting follows the decision made at the unconference.

The board has a handful of members; the exact number has varied over the years. We don't elect a chairman. The board appoints its own chairman at its first meeting after the election.

### The current election process

Before the election starts, Crispers who volunteer to run for the board of directors put their name up for consideration on a dedicated wall somewhere. Often, there is some flattering going on to encourage people to volunteer.

When it is time for the election, all Crispers gather in a big circle. One of the Unconference facilitators manages the election process, where we elect one board member at a time, using closed voting and relative majority.

For each seat on the board:

1.  Any Crisper may nominate any Crisper.
2.  Nominees who accept the nomination are given the opportunity to offer a short motivation for why to vote for them.
3.  Each Crisper casts a vote by writing the name of their favourite candidate on a piece of paper, and putting it in a basket in the middle of the circle.
4.  When everyone has voted, 2-3 people count the votes in front of the group. The candidate with the largest number of votes is announced the winner without further ado.

We repeat this process until all seats are filled.

Since we vote for one seat at a time, we have the possibility to vote for a strong team. Let's say the first person elected is an intuitive agile coach, then, maybe, people will vote for a thoughtful programmer in the next round to balance the team.
In practice, the board usually ends up with a mix of backgrounds, for example programmers and agile coaches.

### History and future direction

This process has evolved from trying lots of different ones, here are two examples of former processes:

-   One man nomination committee that talked to everyone to hear their opinion and then put together a suggestion that always got elected.
    -   pros: put together a team.
    -   cons: lack of transparency, didn't scale
-   Closed vote, the 3 people with most votes became the board.
    -   pros: easy and fair, scales
    -   cons: still lack of transparency, not possible to vote for a team only individuals, not all different views would get represented

We are currently looking to change how we elect the board to one based on the Sociocracy election process to get more reasoning about the composition and feedback to the nominated people.
